How to Store Immigration Documents on Your Phone

By iCyberAI LLC · Updated October 31, 2026 · 5 min read

Short Answer

Set a strong passcode, scan each document front and back, and put the copies in a locked app that stores them on the device, not in your camera roll or a cloud folder. Then delete the camera-roll photos and test that everything opens in airplane mode. ICE Ready is a free app built for exactly this.

A phone copy is a backup, not a replacement. If you're a noncitizen 18 or older, federal law still requires you to carry your original registration document. See what to carry.

Forgot your wallet? Why the copy matters

It happens all the time. You rush out the door and your wallet is still on the nightstand, or in the pants or jacket you wore yesterday. Then you get pulled over or stopped.

A photo of your license or green card isn't a legal substitute for the card in most states. Police generally won't accept it as the license itself, and a few states that issue official mobile driver's licenses accept only those. What a clear copy does give the officer is your full name, date of birth, and license or A-number to look up on the spot. Being able to show who you are and that your status is valid can make the difference between a warning, a ticket, and being held while they confirm your identity.

If you get a ticket for not having the card with you, many states will reduce or dismiss it when you later show you had a valid license. Check your state's rules, and see which states accept digital IDs at traffic stops.

Which documents to copy

  • Green card (front and back), work permit (EAD), visa, I-94
  • USCIS receipt and approval notices (I-797), and your A-number
  • Passport and passport card, birth certificate, naturalization or citizenship certificate
  • Driver's license or state ID, Social Security card
  • Your children's birth certificates and any custody or caregiver documents
  • An immigration attorney's name and phone number

Step by step

1. Set a strong passcode, and consider turning off Face ID and fingerprint unlock

Use at least six digits. iPhones and modern Android phones encrypt their storage when a passcode is set, so that protection comes from the phone itself. Face and fingerprint unlock are convenient, but in some situations they're easier to compel than a passcode.

2. Scan each document, front and back

Lay the document flat in good light. On iPhone, the Notes app's document scanner gives clean, flat images. On Android, Google Drive's scan feature works, but save the result to the device, not the cloud. Check that every number is readable.

3. Move the copies into a locked, offline app

4. Delete the camera-roll copies

Once the copies are in your locked app, delete them from Photos, then empty Recently Deleted. Otherwise they can sync to iCloud or Google Photos and show up when someone scrolls your phone.

5. Test it in airplane mode

Turn on airplane mode, open the app, and pull up each document. If it doesn't open with zero bars, it won't help you in a basement, a rural stop, or a detention intake room.

6. Leave a paper set with someone you trust

Phones get taken. A family member or friend who holds paper copies can get them to your attorney if you're detained.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to keep a photo of my green card on my phone?

Yes, with a strong passcode and a locked app, not the camera roll. It's a backup, not a substitute for carrying the original.

Can I show a document without unlocking my whole phone?

Use an app with its own lock and auto-lock, and hold the screen up instead of handing the phone over.

Does ICE Ready upload my documents anywhere?

No. There's no account, no server, and no cloud. Copies stay on your device.
