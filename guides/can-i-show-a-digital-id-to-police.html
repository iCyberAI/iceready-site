Can I Show a Digital ID or Photo of My License to Police?

By iCyberAI LLC · Last checked October 31, 2026 · 5 min read

Short Answer

A photo of your license usually isn't a legal substitute for the card. It does give the officer your license number to look up, which can turn a bad stop into a manageable one. A state-issued mobile driver's license is different, and a few states now accept it at traffic stops. For green cards, federal law requires the original.

Laws change fast here. This page was last checked on October 31, 2026. Before relying on it, confirm with your state's DMV or highway patrol using the official links below.

States where police accept mobile IDs

Only states confirmed through official sources are listed. If yours isn't here, assume you need the physical card and check your state DMV.

MontanaMontana Highway Patrol accepts Montana Mobile IDs and other states' mobile IDs at traffic stops. Troopers can't ask for a mobile ID. You have to offer it, and you never have to hand over your phone. Other Montana agencies may not accept it yet. Montana DOJ
ColoradoColorado State Patrol has accepted the Colorado Digital ID since November 30, 2020, and many local agencies do too, but not all. The state says you still have to carry your physical license. myColorado
GeorgiaSince July 1, 2025 (HB 296), drivers may present a Georgia digital license to police. Until July 1, 2027, agencies may accept it but don't have to, so carry the physical card. After that date, all Georgia law enforcement must be able to read it.
Most other statesAbout 20 states issue mobile IDs, many in Apple or Google Wallet, but mainly for TSA checkpoints. Most police departments still require the physical card.

Green cards, work permits, and I-94s

This part is federal and the same in every state. Noncitizens 18 and older must carry their original registration document (green card, EAD, or I-94) at all times under 8 U.S.C. §1304(e). A photo or copy doesn't satisfy that requirement. It's still worth having, because it gives you the A-number, card number, and expiration date if the card is forgotten, lost, or taken. Passport cards and proof of citizenship

U.S. citizens aren't required to carry proof of citizenship. If you want something wallet-sized that proves it, a U.S. passport card is proof of identity and citizenship. A REAL ID or regular driver's license is not proof of citizenship.

Forgot your wallet? What to do at the stop

  1. Tell the officer calmly that you have a valid license but left it at home.
  2. Offer your name, date of birth, and license number. Hold up your phone copy without handing over an unlocked phone.
  3. If you get a ticket for not having the card on you, ask whether it can be dismissed if you show your valid license later. Many states allow that.
  4. If you're a noncitizen, you have the right to remain silent about where you were born and your immigration status.

Frequently asked questions

Will police accept a photo of my driver's license?

Generally not as the license itself, but it helps them look up and confirm your license.

Is a mobile driver's license the same as a photo?

No. An mDL is issued and verified by your state. A screenshot is not.

Can I show a photo of my green card instead of the card?

It's a backup, not a legal replacement. The original is required for noncitizens 18 and older.

Does a passport card prove citizenship?

Yes, it proves both identity and U.S. citizenship.
