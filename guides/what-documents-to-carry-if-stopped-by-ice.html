What Documents to Carry If You're Stopped by ICE

By iCyberAI LLC · Updated October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

Short Answer

If you're a noncitizen 18 or older with valid immigration papers, federal law says you must carry the original document: green card, work permit (EAD), or I-94. U.S. citizens aren't required to carry anything. Never carry false documents. A copy on your phone is a smart backup in case the original is lost or taken, but it doesn't replace the original.

What federal law requires

Section 264(e) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (8 U.S.C. §1304(e)) requires every noncitizen 18 or older to carry their registration document "at all times." Not carrying it is a misdemeanor: a fine of up to $100, up to 30 days in jail, or both. The law has been on the books for decades. DHS and CBP put out public reminders about it in 2025.

The statute refers to the card itself. It doesn't say "copy" or "other evidence," so most immigration attorneys advise that a photocopy or phone photo does not satisfy the requirement.

Never show or carry false documents. That can turn a stop into a criminal case. If you don't have valid papers, you have the right to remain silent. You don't have to say where you were born or how you entered the U.S.

What many legal aid groups say not to carry

Several know-your-rights trainings advise people without valid U.S. status not to carry documents from another country, such as a foreign passport or national ID (cédula). Those documents can be used as evidence of where you're from. Every situation is different, so ask an immigration attorney or a local legal aid organization what's right for you.

Why a phone copy still matters

Originals get lost, damaged, or held during an arrest, and replacing a green card can take months. A secure copy on your phone helps you:

  • Prove your status quickly if the original was left at home or taken.
  • Help a family member or attorney who needs your A-number, receipt numbers, or expiration dates.
  • Start a replacement faster with the card details in hand.
  • Show U.S. citizenship if you're a citizen who's afraid of being misidentified.

Where you keep that copy matters. Cloud storage like Google Drive won't load without a signal. A screenshot in your camera roll means unlocking your phone and handing it over open. Here's how to store document copies on your phone safely and offline.

Before you're ever stopped

  1. Make copies of every immigration and identity document, front and back.
  2. Leave one paper set with a trusted person who can reach it if you're detained.
  3. Keep a second set on your phone in a locked, offline app.
  4. Write an attorney's number on paper. Your phone may be taken.
  5. Switch your phone to passcode-only. Face ID and fingerprint unlock are easier to compel.

Frequently asked questions

Do green card holders have to carry their green card at all times?

Yes. Noncitizens 18 and older must carry their registration document at all times under 8 U.S.C. §1304(e).

Is a photo of my green card on my phone enough?

Generally no. It's a strong backup, not a legal substitute for the original card.

Do U.S. citizens have to carry proof of citizenship?

No. There's no federal requirement, though some citizens choose to carry a passport card.

What should I never carry?

False documents of any kind. Ask an attorney about foreign documents if you don't have valid U.S. status.
